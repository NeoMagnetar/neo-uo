# Combat Systems

## Purpose

Define the combat identity for Neo UO and protect it from drifting back toward ordinary shard behavior.

## Player Experience Goal

Combat should feel:

- tense
- readable
- more consequential than stock UO
- survivable but dangerous
- capable of creating pursuit and aftermath rather than instant clean reset

## Design Position

Neo UO combat should not be built around who can most quickly erase the other player in a short exchange.

It should be built around:

- pressure
- wound creation
- incomplete recovery
- escape windows
- survival decisions
- organized group coverage without unlimited stack efficiency

## Core Combat Doctrine

### Law 1 — Survival Matters More Than Fast Deletion

Combat should create meaningful states before it creates death.

### Law 2 — Drawn-Out Does Not Mean Spongey

The goal is not meaningless HP bloat. The goal is lasting consequence, better readability, and more space for tactical response.

### Law 3 — Grouping Must Be Strong but Not Mindless

Groups should win through coverage, coordination, and role use more than raw dense stacking.

### Law 4 — Combat Must Spill Into World Behavior

A fight should often lead into:

- retreat
- pursuit
- recovery
- concealment
- rescue
- or renewed contact

## Intended Combat Feel

The intended feeling is:

- a serious fight is dangerous
- getting pressured does not always mean instant death
- escaping at low health is meaningful
- staying engaged while wounded is risky
- re-entering combat after damage is not trivial

## Main Combat Levers

Neo UO’s combat identity will likely be shaped through some combination of:

- higher practical survivability
- lower burst finishing
- weaker instant reset healing
- slower return to full readiness
- role differentiation
- anti-zerg efficiency pressure
- burden and world-state consequences after the fight

The exact balance between these levers must be tested rather than assumed.

## Anti-Zerg Doctrine

The project’s current direction favors a land-pressure or local spell-density concept.

### Intent

Discourage stacked spell spam from dense groups while preserving the value of organized teamplay.

### Desired Effect

- spread positioning becomes smarter
- sequencing matters more
- coordination still wins
- mindless caster clustering loses efficiency

### Current Rule

Groups should gain coverage, control, and layered pressure more than simple linear deletion strength.

## Offensive vs Defensive Scaling

Long-lived characters should become:

- harder to kill
- more resilient
- more versatile
- better prepared

They should not become:

- uncontestable delete engines
- safe from organized threat
- able to erase newer players with no meaningful counterplay

## Role Expression Direction

Over time, combat should support broader character identity:

- hunters
- scouts
- resilient frontliners
- survival specialists
- utility-heavy builds
- support or control variants later if healthy

This should come through utility and survivability more than unchecked damage inflation.

## MVP Combat Version

The MVP combat pass should aim to prove only a few things:

- fights are more deliberate than stock UO
- instant reset loops are weakened
- escaping under pressure is meaningful
- combat outcome spills into recovery and movement choices

### MVP Must Not Try To Solve

- full endgame balance
- full role ecosystem
- every edge case between veteran and new-player parity
- full arena-world cross-tuning

## Later Expansion Version

Later versions may include:

- deeper role expression
- deity-linked utility
- broader item-based specialization
- advanced anti-zerg tuning
- richer wound / mobility interactions
- broader political consequences tied to combat outcomes

## Design Risks

### Risk: Combat Becomes Slow but Empty

If damage is lowered without meaningful pressure, fights become dull.

Mitigation:

- preserve danger
- preserve pursuit consequences
- preserve non-damage pressure

### Risk: Defensive Power Outruns Threat

If survivability rises too far, veterans may become too difficult to challenge.

Mitigation:

- constrain offensive and defensive scaling
- preserve coordinated threat windows
- use bounty and exposure pressure later

### Risk: Anti-Zerg Tuning Feels Arbitrary

If group penalties feel unexplained, players may reject them.

Mitigation:

- tie them to in-world logic
- keep them learnable
- test gradually

## Implementation Notes

The first combat pass should be conservative and directional.
Do not try to finalize balance before the first real local tests.

Useful first prototype questions:

- what changes most affect practical TTK?
- how much weaker should immediate healing resets be?
- does combat still feel intense if players survive longer but recover less fully?

## Open Questions

- should survivability come more from health increase or damage reduction?
- what first anti-zerg implementation is easiest to prototype?
- how much should combat pacing differ from stock UO in early tests?
- how soon should burden and recovery interact directly with combat?

## Current Direction

Neo UO combat should be slower, more dangerous in aftermath, and more likely to create survival situations rather than instant clean kill-reset loops.
