# Progression

## Purpose

Define how Neo UO handles skill growth, character breadth, backup-character development, safe-vs-dangerous training, and long-term capability.

## Player Experience Goal

Progression should feel:

- meaningful
- slower than disposable loops
- rewarding enough to sustain interest
- tied to dangerous world participation
- broad enough to support long-lived capable characters
- painful to lose, but not impossible to recover from at the player level

## Design Position

Neo UO should not make replacement trivial.
It also should not make one character death equivalent to losing all ability to play.

Progression must therefore support both:

- valuable long-lived mains
- and weaker continuity characters

## Core Progression Doctrine

### Law 1 — Dangerous Growth Must Outpace Safe Growth

The world should reward risk with faster advancement.

### Law 2 — Safe Growth May Exist, But Must Be Inferior

Town or protected-area advancement can support continuity and low-attention play, but it must not outcompete real-world exposure.

### Law 3 — Breadth Is Valuable

Neo UO supports the idea that characters may grow broader over time rather than living in ultra-narrow disposable templates.

### Law 4 — Mastery Must Take Time

The project should preserve the feeling that long-term capability is earned.

## Skill Cap Direction

The project direction allows for the possibility of a skill cap above classic baseline assumptions.

### Why

- encourage broader capable characters
- allow long-lived survivors to develop fuller identities
- support solo self-sufficiency in some cases
- make veteran lives feel more lived-in

### Constraint

Higher cap does not mean easy mastery.
The deeper or broader the build becomes, the harder additional gains should feel.

## Safe vs Dangerous Training

### Dangerous Training

Should be:

- faster
- more meaningful
- more interesting
- exposed to PvP, creature, and logistical pressure

### Safe Training

Should be:

- slower
- more limited
- more continuity-supportive than dominance-supportive
- appropriate for backups and low-attention sessions

### Project Intent

A player who risks the world should advance more meaningfully than a player who remains in protected loops.

## Macroing Doctrine

Neo UO should not treat every skill as equally unattended-trainable.

### Acceptable Direction

Some baseline or lower-pressure progression may occur through slower, limited, safe activity.

### Unacceptable Direction

A player should not be able to unattended-macro their way into full real-world competence with no meaningful exposure.

## Backup Character Progression

Backup characters are one of the most important support systems in the project.

### Why

- death of a main should not fully eject the player
- developing alternate lives creates continuity
- a weaker successor keeps the player active after loss

### Constraint

Backup growth must not trivialize death.

The main should still matter more:

- in strength
- in prestige
- in utility
- in social weight

## Experimental Directions

The following ideas have been discussed and should remain preserved for future testing:

### Higher Skill Cap Variants

- go meaningfully above 700 total skills
- allow broader hybrid characters over very long-term development
- make deeper specialization above baseline increasingly difficult
- allow self-sufficient field-hardened builds without allowing effortless mastery of everything

### Survivor Build Philosophy

- a long-lived player may become more versatile, hardy, and capable
- growth should support survival, logistics, and utility
- growth should not mainly become raw burst escalation

### Backup Character Support Variants

- several developing characters may exist at once
- town-based or protected training may help maintain weak continuity characters
- a dead main should push the player into a weaker fallback, not total restart emptiness

### Training Environment Variants

- training in the dangerous world should be meaningfully faster
- training in towns or safer zones may be heavily reduced
- some skills may require live-world context and cannot be meaningfully macroed in total safety

### Time-Value Philosophy

- long-term characters should feel lived-in
- replacement should be possible, but slow enough that life still matters

These are preserved experiment targets, not locked economy or skill rules yet.

## MVP Version

The MVP progression pass should likely define:

- safe-vs-dangerous gain philosophy
- first cap direction assumptions
- early anti-abuse rules
- early backup-character boundaries

### MVP Goal

Prove that slower progression plus continuity can coexist without flattening death.

## Later Expansion Version

Later versions may add:

- more advanced skill-cap shaping
- deeper training environment distinctions
- stronger role differentiation
- more complex support for broad character identities
- additional utility and deity-linked growth layers

## Design Risks

### Risk: Progression Too Slow

Players may lose interest before they feel identity growth.

Mitigation:

- preserve meaningful milestones
- ensure gains feel visible enough even if slower

### Risk: Progression Too Fast

Death and continuity lose meaning.

Mitigation:

- preserve time value
- reward danger more than safety
- avoid fast unattended mastery

### Risk: Backups Flatten Main Loss

If backups are too strong or too easy, permadeath loses teeth.

Mitigation:

- keep backup growth materially weaker
- preserve prestige and readiness gaps

## Implementation Notes

The first implementation pass should define:

- progression rate philosophy
- account-level backup boundaries
- what safe training actually means in rules terms
- what skills or categories are restricted from easy unattended growth

## Open Questions

- what is the first practical safe-vs-dangerous gain ratio to test?
- how far above traditional cap should the long-term model go, if at all?
- which skills are most dangerous to leave unattended-trainable?
- how many continuity characters is healthy?

## Current Direction

Neo UO progression should support broader long-lived characters, meaningful dangerous-world advancement, and weaker continuity lives without reducing death to a trivial inconvenience.
