# Design Constitution

## Law 1 — Life Must Matter

Character death must carry real consequence.

## Law 2 — Death Must Hurt, But Not Eject the Player

A character’s story may end, but the player must still have viable continuity through backup structures and ongoing participation.

## Law 3 — Survival Power Must Outscale Burst Power

Long-lived characters should become harder to kill, more resilient, and richer in utility more than they become raw deletion machines.

## Law 4 — Risk Must Accelerate Growth

Meaningful progression should occur faster in dangerous environments than in safe ones.

## Law 5 — Groups Gain Coverage, Not Unlimited Efficiency

Grouping is valid and important, but dense stacking and brainless zerging should face diminishing returns and counter-pressure.

## Law 6 — Tracking Must Provide Evidence, Not Certainty

Hunts should be driven by clues, recent movement, prediction, and geography rather than direct target arrows.

## Law 7 — Utility and Counterplay Are Preferred Before Blunt Nerfs

Balance should first look for counterplay, opportunity cost, positional pressure, or role answers before direct flattening.

## Law 8 — Shared Combat Grammar Across Modes

World mode and arena mode should feel like different stakes applied to the same combat language.

## Law 9 — PvE Feeds Exposure, Economy, and Conflict

Monsters matter, but they are not the core identity of the game.

## Law 10 — Territory Must Gain Meaning Over Time

Housing, strongholds, roads, and travel zones should become strategic realities rather than passive backdrop.

## Law 11 — Recovery Must Preserve Tension Without Creating Dead Time

Escaping a fight should save a life, not erase the consequences of the fight. Recovery must matter without turning normal play into waiting.

## Law 12 — Social Consequence Must Exceed Simple Kill Resolution

Capture, ransom, rescue, negotiation, and political leverage should exist where they strengthen the world.
